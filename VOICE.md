# VOICE.md — how Jake sounds (law)

Sources: Jake's chat transcripts (Notion) and his redline of `redline-01`
(jaique vault, `x/Attachments/redline-01.md`). Applies to everything
written on the site, in any register, and to how agents write to Jake.
Simple, accessible, effective beats original. Truth beats punch.

## The patterns (confirmed against his transcripts)

1. **Plain talk beats cleverness.** "The volume buttons are backwards,
   which is weird." State the fact, react flatly, move on.
2. **Feelings stated flatly.** "That doesn't feel great." "I don't care
   for it." "That was scary." Small words carry the weight.
3. **Both hands on the table.** Claims come with their counterweight.
   "I'm proud of what we put out, but the team is still kind of burnt out."
4. **Household metaphors.** "Big dumb buttons." "Something you'd have in
   your kitchen and not be embarrassed by if you had guests over."
   Metaphors come from life, never from literature.
5. **Story order, not thesis order.** So... and then... from there. Build
   momentum chronologically and let the point land on its own.
6. **Understated enthusiasm.** "Pretty cool." "Kind of fun." "A big win."
   The excitement is real; the words stay small.
7. **Hedges kept in.** "I wouldn't say I staged a coup, but..." Corrections
   happen in-flight and stay visible.
8. **Questions to explore.** "Is it a big clock? Does it show the weather?"
9. **Zero jargon.** "Get people on board," never "drive alignment."
   "Put the complication on the back end," never "abstract the complexity."

## Banned moves

- Aphorism endings ("We retired the whiteboard.")
- "Not X. Y." reversals ("A thinker, not a follower.")
- Em dashes, anywhere. Use a period, comma, or colon.
- Abstractions doing human things ("hardware keeps you honest").
  Inanimate subject with a human verb: name the person instead.
- "quietly / actually / simply" + verb as drama.
- Fragment. Chains. For effect.
- Passive voice: find the actor, make them the subject.
- Sentences that start with a Wh- word: restructure.
- "Here's what / the thing is" throat-clearing: cut to the point.
- "Not X, it's Y" contrasts: state Y directly.
- Three consecutive sentences of matching length: break one.
- Paragraphs that end on a punchy one-liner: vary it.
- Vague declaratives ("The implications are significant"): name the
  specific implication.
- Narrator-from-a-distance ("Nobody designed this"): put the reader in
  the scene.
- Meta-joiners ("The rest of this essay..."): delete, let it move.

## Words and phrases

Specific words Jake has flagged as confusing or off. One line each: the
word, why, what to say instead. Add to this list; do not argue with it.
It applies to section labels, table headers and machine strings, not
only prose.

- "green light" — collides with the company name. Say "approval," "the
  go-ahead," "the yes."
- "sound" as a label meaning correct ("Sound: …") — reads as audio. Say
  "right," or label the section "Keep."
- "spine" as a metaphor for structure ("this page is the spine of the
  case study") — say "structure," "outline," or name the thing ("the
  table the case study is built on").
- Crit jargon as headings ("Push," "Sound," "Took"). Section labels are
  plain words a stranger reads once. The review shape is Checks · Keep ·
  Change · Wrong · Next.

## Rulings (from the redline session, 2026-08-13)

- Spoken intensifiers are Jake's: "pretty much," "really," "kind of,"
  "pretty cool" live. There is no blanket adverb ban.
- Truth over punch. Never sharpen a claim past what happened. If a fact
  is unclear, ask Jake; he said "if you want clarity, you need only ask."
- Sincere and common beats clever and original. "The sky is the limit"
  and "stay tuned" are allowed when meant.
- Speaking as Greenlight ("we," "our families") is allowed where it shows
  he's invested in the team.
- Swearing is allowed everywhere, site verbatims included. Jake: "swearing
  is an intimate act that signals authenticity." Use it like he does,
  where it matters, not as decoration.
- The machine's ALL-CAPS register keeps its caps and terseness, and its
  wording obeys the ban list too (no em dashes, no cleverness).

## Exemplars (his lines, use as few-shot)

**Was:** A kid told me the numbers meant nothing. So I built the
understanding — in code.
**Jake:** Regardless of the layout or data, kids told us they didn't
understand, so we got on their level.

**Was:** Design and engineering stopped being a handoff and started being
the same motion.
**Jake:** That's pretty much how I work now. I designed the interaction,
specced the logic and taste, and refined with the data team so we could
generate at scale. There wasn't really a handoff anywhere in it.

**Was:** But the outcome I keep is quieter than a metric.
**Jake:** The part I keep coming back to is more basic than the numbers.
[...] They have a conversation about it now. That's pretty cool.

**Was:** *Persuasion is a deliverable.*
**Jake:** *The prototype sold the vision.* Most people can't imagine a
product from a description, and that's fair. So I build something they
can react to.

**Was:** We didn't ship another smart display. We retired the whiteboard.
**Jake:** We're not stopping here. Although we shipped fast and have
experience gaps to fill, that's the mission: continue to listen to our
families and provide new solutions that work for them. Now that we have a
platform for that, the sky is the limit. Stay tuned.

**Was:** Jake writes the brief, judges every return and owns the taste.
**Jake:** I set the course and judge what comes back from the crew of AI
agents. This is a peek inside what they're up to, for both you and me.
