  Compliance means we can never tell a kid what to buy. So the product plays
  their own reasoning back to them: *based on your answers, this might be
  worth considering*, or *this doesn&rsquo;t look like a fit.* Honestly, that
  constraint made the design better. It pushed us to teach kids how to think
  about the decision instead of handing them one.
</Section>

<Section no="03" label="Built it myself" title="Engineering said the graph was too hard. I disagreed.">
  I wanted an interactive performance graph: press and hold, scrub across
  time, feel a haptic tick at each interval. Engineering came back and said
  it was too hard to build. Every trading app has this interaction, and
  that answer annoyed me enough to go build it myself.

  <Plate cap="Plate 03 · performance graph" tag="haptic scrub" caption="Scrub it, and each interval ticks. The shipped SwiftUI version does this under your thumb, with real haptics.">
    <ScrubGraph />
  </Plate>

  I paired with an engineer who gave me access to the real repo and a
  playground inside it. Using AI coding tools, I wrote the interaction in
  SwiftUI against our actual codebase, state by state, until it felt right.
  Most of that code shipped. It was the first haptic feedback anywhere in
  the app, and the first time the graph felt alive under my thumb.

  That&rsquo;s pretty much how I work now. I designed the interaction,
  specced the logic and taste, and refined with the data team so we could
  generate at scale. There wasn&rsquo;t really a handoff anywhere in it.
</Section>

<Section no="04" label="The call" title="What didn't ship.">
  The most ambitious idea, *Grows with You*, didn&rsquo;t make it. It would
  have read the whole picture of a young investor, their experience, their
  spending, their family&rsquo;s financial context, and leveled the entire
  interface up with them: language, data density, and complexity all rising
  as the kid grew into it.

  We couldn&rsquo;t afford it in the time we had, so we made the hard call
  and designed for the newest investor instead, simple enough for everyone
  on day one. I still believe in the adaptive version, and I&rsquo;d like to
  build it someday. For that timeline, shipping the simple version that
  worked was the right call.
</Section>
